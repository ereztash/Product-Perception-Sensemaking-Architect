## Neta challenge to the initiating framing

The ByteByteGo video is useful because it starts from recurring pressure and proposes mechanisms. It also risks a mechanism-collapse shortcut:

- high reads ≠ cache is automatically justified;
- high writes ≠ queue/LSM is automatically justified;
- replication ≠ sufficient availability;
- CDN ≠ sufficient global-latency solution;
- observability ≠ logging/metrics volume;
- slow query ≠ index is always the root fix;
- large scale ≠ sharding is justified;
- named pattern ≠ architectural fitness.

Therefore pattern names remain candidate mechanisms, not research objects or conclusions.

## R&D research objects

### 1. WORKLOAD_PRESSURE
Observed or forecast demand that makes an architecture property material.

Candidate dimensions:
- read/write ratio;
- throughput and peak/burst behavior;
- latency distribution, not only average;
- data size and growth rate;
- payload/event size;
- geographic distribution;
- concurrency/contention;
- workload heterogeneity;
- change frequency/churn.

### 2. REQUIRED_PROPERTY
The user/business-relevant property the architecture must preserve or achieve.

Candidate dimensions:
- latency;
- throughput;
- availability;
- durability/data-loss tolerance;
- consistency/freshness;
- recoverability/RTO/RPO;
- security/compliance;
- cost envelope;
- operability;
- maintainability/evolvability.

Where service behavior is measurable, express this as an SLI/SLO or another bounded target rather than `fast`, `highly available`, or `scalable`.

### 3. BOTTLENECK_EVIDENCE
Observed evidence that the current structure is failing or approaching a material limit.

Examples:
- saturation/queueing;
- DB query plan or I/O bottleneck;
- cache miss behavior;
- lock/contention;
- network/region latency;
- replication lag;
- failure/recovery trace;
- resource/cost curve;
- operational incident/toil.

Absence of measurement does not prove absence of a problem, but it lowers authority for irreversible interventions.

### 4. CANDIDATE_MECHANISM
A bounded structural mechanism that could satisfy the required property under the observed workload.

Examples generated by the initiating corpus:
- caching;
- asynchronous queue/worker processing;
- write-optimized storage/LSM structures;
- replication/failover;
- load balancing;
- CDN/edge;
- block/object storage selection;
- metrics/logs/traces and SLO-aware observability;
- indexing;
- partitioning/sharding.

Mechanism name is not a recommendation.

### 5. MECHANISM_COUNTERINDICATION
Conditions under which a candidate mechanism creates more decision cost/risk than value.

Examples:
- cache staleness/invalidation or low hit ratio;
- asynchronous work where immediate consistency/confirmation is required;
- replication where write consistency or failover semantics are unresolved;
- sharding before single-node/index/query limits are evidenced;
- distributed decomposition where coordination/operational burden dominates scale gain.

### 6. TRADEOFF_VECTOR
What property improves and what cost/constraint is accepted.

Do not collapse tradeoffs into one score. Preserve at least:
- performance;
- reliability;
- consistency;
- cost;
- operational complexity;
- changeability/reversibility.

### 7. OPERATIONAL_BURDEN
The ongoing human/runtime burden added by the mechanism: monitoring, incident response, deployment, data repair, migration, debugging, coordination, staffing/skill requirements.

This is distinct from implementation effort.

### 8. MIGRATION_AND_REVERSAL
How the current state can move to the candidate state, how success/failure is observed, and what makes reversal difficult.

This extends the existing discriminator's `MIGRATION_PATH` and `REVERSIBILITY`; it does not replace them.

## Research questions

### Q1 — Decision grammar
Does `WORKLOAD_PRESSURE → REQUIRED_PROPERTY → BOTTLENECK_EVIDENCE → CANDIDATE_MECHANISM → COUNTERINDICATION/TRADEOFF → MIGRATION` change architecture decisions beyond v0's current objects?

### Q2 — Quality attributes / SLOs
Which nonfunctional requirements must be first-class before a technical mechanism is considered, and when is an explicit SLO worth the cost?

### Q3 — Mechanism triggers
For each high-ROI mechanism (cache, queue, replication, CDN, indexing, partitioning), what measured conditions support considering it and what neighboring conditions should make it not fire?

### Q4 — Operational burden
Does making ongoing operational burden explicit prevent technically valid but globally miscalibrated architecture decisions?

### Q5 — Reversibility
Which mechanisms are particularly migration-sensitive or hard to reverse, and what discriminating fact should be bought before commitment?

### Q6 — Coverage boundary
Which knowledge belongs in the Architecture capability, which stays external/borrowed, and which decisions remain R&D/Neta/OWNER/REPO/ENVIRONMENT/FIELD-owned?

## Source strategy

Priority is by evidential function, not popularity.

1. Primary architecture/SRE frameworks and authoritative engineering texts — decision principles, quality attributes, failure/operations boundaries.
2. Primary technology documentation — concrete mechanism semantics and counterindications.
3. Strong empirical/postmortem/benchmark evidence — behavior, failure modes, scaling limits, migration costs.
4. ByteByteGo and similar technical videos — hypothesis discovery, compact mental models, candidate mechanism inventory.
5. Community/interview-prep material — discovery only unless independently verified.

## Pass 1 — initiating corpus and primary framing

### V1 — ByteByteGo: 8 Most Important System Design Concepts You Should Know
Role: hypothesis generator / pressure→mechanism primer.

Observed pressure/mechanism families:
- high read volume → caching;
- high write volume → asynchronous processing plus write-optimized storage;
- downtime/failure → replication/failover/load balancing;
- global latency → CDN/edge;
- large data → storage-model choice;
- performance uncertainty → observability;
- slow database queries → indexing;
- extreme database scale → partitioning/sharding, explicitly framed as a high-complexity/last-resort move.

Transfer ceiling: does not establish that any mechanism is justified in a particular system without workload, constraints, evidence and tradeoff analysis.

### P1 — Google SRE: Service Level Objectives
Source: https://sre.google/sre-book/service-level-objectives/

Decision-relevant transfer:
- begin from user-relevant behavior, not merely easy-to-measure infrastructure metrics;
- define measurable targets and validity conditions;
- heterogeneous workload classes may require different objectives;
- 100% reliability can be undesirable and expensive;
- reliability target selection carries product/business tradeoffs.

Candidate delta: `REQUIRED_PROPERTY/SLO` should be explicit enough to constrain architecture mechanisms when reliability/performance is material.

### P2 — AWS Well-Architected Framework
Source: https://docs.aws.amazon.com/wellarchitected/latest/userguide/waf.html

Decision-relevant transfer:
- architecture decisions trade operational excellence, security, reliability, performance efficiency, cost and sustainability according to business needs;
- the same architecture move can be rational in a mission-critical workload and irrational in a low-cost development workload.

Candidate delta: preserve a multi-dimensional `TRADEOFF_VECTOR`; do not optimize architecture against one quality attribute.

### P3 — Azure Architecture Center: Design principles
Source: https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/azure/architecture/guide/design-principles/

Decision-relevant transfer:
- start from business needs;
- design for self-healing/failure, redundancy, scale-out, partitioning around limits, operations and evolution;
- define RTO/SLO-like objectives where relevant;
- perform failure-mode analysis rather than assuming redundancy equals reliability.

Candidate delta: `OPERATIONAL_BURDEN`, `FAILURE_MODE/PATH`, and `EVOLUTION` deserve explicit testing in historical cases.

### P4 — Martin Kleppmann: Designing Data-Intensive Applications
Source: https://martin.kleppmann.com/2017/03/27/designing-data-intensive-applications.html

Decision-relevant transfer:
- reliability, scalability and maintainability are enduring properties;
- technologies should be selected by strengths/weaknesses and tradeoffs around consistency, scalability, fault tolerance and complexity, not by buzzword/pattern availability.

Candidate delta: reinforces mechanism comparison and complexity cost, but does not by itself prove a new discriminator is required.

## Pass 1 synthesis

The initiating video should **not** be internalized as `8 concepts to know`.

The stronger transferable grammar is:

```text
TELOS / USER NEED
→ WORKLOAD_PRESSURE
→ REQUIRED_PROPERTY / SLO
→ BOTTLENECK_EVIDENCE
→ 2–3 CANDIDATE_MECHANISMS
→ COUNTERINDICATIONS + TRADEOFF_VECTOR + OPERATIONAL_BURDEN
→ CHEAPEST DISCRIMINATING FACT
→ BOUNDED ARCHITECTURE DECISION
→ MIGRATION / REVERSAL CONDITION
```

This is a candidate adaptation, not a canonical change.

## Promotion / falsification rules

A candidate object earns adaptation only if, on historical cases, adding it changes at least one of:
- chosen mechanism;
- decision to defer/anti-build;
- authority handoff;
- migration path;
- reversal condition;
- required evidence before commitment.

It does **not** count if it only adds terminology, explanation quality, diagrams or post-hoc rationale.

### Positive controls
Cases where a naive pressure→pattern mapping should be wrong or incomplete, e.g. a read-heavy system where caching is unnecessary/harmful, or a scaling complaint whose actual bottleneck is query/index design rather than sharding.

### Neighboring non-fire cases
Small/simple workloads whose current architecture already meets the required property and where additional distributed infrastructure should not be recommended.

### Falsifier
If 8–15 historical cases show no unique material decision delta from the candidate objects compared with Architecture Decision Discriminator v0 + generic Scaffold + REPO/ENVIRONMENT evidence, do not adapt v0. Keep the knowledge external.
