The purpose of this register is not to survey epistemology exhaustively. It retains only sources that can plausibly alter R&D's live capability decision.

| ID | Source | Relevant distinction | Candidate R&D delta |
|---|---|---|---|
| EPI-01 | Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy — Evidence | Evidence is normatively tied to what one is justified/reasonable in believing; evidence is not merely information present in context. | Sharpen evidence→claim support semantics. |
| EPI-02 | SEP — The Epistemic Basing Relation (2026 revision) | Possessing a good reason is different from holding a belief *because of* that reason. | Detect post-hoc rationalization / evidence co-presence without decision lineage. |
| EPI-03 | SEP — Higher-Order Evidence | Evidence about one's evidence, reliability, bias, reasoning performance or evidential situation can affect first-order confidence. | Explicitly represent evidence about the reliability of an inference/model/agent/run. |
| EPI-04 | SEP — Reliabilist Epistemology | Justificatory status may depend on the truth-conduciveness/reliability of the process generating a belief, not only the apparent evidence. | Distinguish artifact-level support from method/process reliability. |
| EPI-05 | SEP — Social Epistemology / Testimony / Disagreement | Testimony, expertise, peer disagreement and group epistemics raise separate justification questions; disagreement can be higher-order evidence. | Better weighting of experts/agents and non-independent agreement. |
| EPI-06 | SEP — Underdetermination of Scientific Theory | Available evidence may support multiple competing hypotheses; correlation and other observations may fail to determine which explanation is correct. | Protect against premature closure when evidence does not discriminate. |
| EPI-07 | SEP — Abduction (2025 revision) | 'Best explanation' is best only relative to a candidate set and explanatory virtues; explanatory superiority does not automatically guarantee truth. | Add candidate-set completeness / missing-alternative check before promoting IBE. |
| EPI-08 | SEP — Risk; Cambridge Philosophy of Science — epistemic risk | Acceptance/rejection and methodological choices carry risks of graded error; standards of evidence can depend on consequences of error and methodological assumptions. | Tie evidence sufficiency to asymmetric error costs without collapsing permission into confidence. |
| EPI-09 | SEP — Bayesian Epistemology | Beliefs can be represented as degrees of belief/credence, with norms for updating in response to evidence. | Potential graded-update discipline where quantified evidence is legitimate. |
| EPI-10 | SEP — Epistemic Utility Arguments | Formal epistemology evaluates epistemic states via accuracy/disutility and decision-theoretic norms. | Possible formalization, but risk of confusing epistemic accuracy with practical decision value. |
| EPI-11 | SEP — The Value of Knowledge | More knowledge/understanding is not automatically valuable merely because it is knowledge; epistemic value is itself contested/multidimensional. | Supports anti-'more knowledge is always better' stance, but may not add an operational rule. |
| VOI-01 | ISPOR Task Force, Value of Information Analysis for Research Decisions (2020) | New evidence is valuable when reducing uncertainty is expected to improve a resource-allocation decision; research itself competes against its cost. | Very close match to R&D 'epistemic effort calibration'. |
| VOI-02 | 'When is enough evidence enough?' (2013) | Separate current best decision from whether more evidence should be gathered and which study to run. | Direct operational scaffold for continue/change-channel/stop. |
| VOI-03 | National Academies/AHRQ materials on Value of Information | If no possible information outcome can change the decision, information value is zero; acquisition and delay costs matter. | Formal support for decision-changing-learning stop rule. |

## Source-level caution

The strongest conceptual fit to the new R&D telos is not classical epistemology alone. The `which uncertainty is worth paying to reduce?` question maps especially closely to **decision analysis / Value of Information**, while epistemology contributes norms for evidence, justification, reliability, defeat, disagreement and uncertainty. This distinction must survive synthesis; do not relabel decision analysis as epistemology for conceptual neatness.
