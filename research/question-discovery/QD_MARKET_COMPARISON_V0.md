# Question Discovery / Decision Framing — Market Comparison v0

Status: `EXTERNAL_MARKET_SCAN · NAMING_INPUT · NOT_CANONICAL`
Date: 2026-09-06

Purpose: compare the candidate capability against adjacent products that solve similar pre-decision / decision-framing problems, and extract naming implications.

## Closest products

### Qzero / Question Zero
Problem framing: costly mistakes happen when money, time or attention are committed before the situation is properly defined.
Core flow: start from the current question, define the real decision, separate evidence from assumptions, isolate the real constraint, then shape decision architecture.
Naming pattern: names the moment before the first real decision. Short coined brand (`Qzero`) + explanatory concept (`Question Zero`).
Relation to us: extremely close to the earlier 'question before the question' framing. This territory is already occupied and should not be copied.

### Frameback
Problem framing: product teams build the wrong thing quickly because solution requests are accepted before returning to the underlying customer problem.
Core flow: detect requested solution, take it back to the problem, pressure-test alternatives against evidence, retain a decision journal.
Naming pattern: coined verb-like movement. The name itself encodes the transformation ('take the request back to its problem').
Relation to us: very close mechanism, but domain-specific to product organizations. Our candidate is cross-domain and selects the controlling question/uncertainty, not only the customer problem.

### DTrio / Decision Framing (Decision Frameworks)
Problem framing: people jump to solutions before defining the decision problem and relevant uncertainties.
Core flow: problem definition, issues, uncertainty table, decision hierarchy, alternatives/strategies, influence diagrams, evaluation.
Naming pattern: brand name plus established category descriptor (`Decision Framing`).
Relation to us: broader than our wedge. DTrio continues through option development/evaluation; our candidate can stop after selecting the question worth resolving next.

### Frame Decisions
Problem framing: high-stakes initiatives are unclear and under-structured before resources are committed.
Core flow: translate intent, compare options, test readiness, surface risks/KPIs/gaps, generate decision brief.
Naming pattern: imperative/action + valued object (`Frame Decisions`).
Relation to us: broader downstream workflow. Our candidate is earlier: decide which question/uncertainty deserves attention before building the full decision frame.

### Alter AI
Problem framing: messy choices make it hard to see the next step.
Core flow: separate facts/pressure/options, find the first assumption to test, compare tradeoffs, choose one next action.
Naming pattern: transformation/outcome word (`Alter`) rather than method name.
Relation to us: overlaps on assumption testing and smallest next test, but focuses on turning messy choices into action rather than selecting the highest-value question.

### Decision Journal
Problem framing: decision reasoning is distorted by bias and hindsight, and learning is lost.
Core flow: capture intent, flag bias, revisit outcome, calibrate.
Naming pattern: object + persistent workflow (`Decision Journal`).
Relation to us: mostly downstream. It assumes a decision is already identified and improves process/learning around it.

### Aivatar Decisions
Problem framing: chat rewards whoever phrases questions well and produces unstructured reasoning for high-stakes calls.
Core flow: decision framing, stakeholders, context, optionality, disqualifiers, next steps.
Naming pattern: brand + object (`Decisions`).
Relation to us: directly acknowledges phrasing-quality failure, but solves it by generating a structured decision brief rather than selecting which question should be resolved first.

### IBM Decision Intelligence
Problem framing: enterprises need governed, auditable decision logic at scale.
Core flow: policies/data/rules/models → governed decision flows → execution/monitoring.
Naming pattern: broad category + outcome (`Decision Intelligence`).
Relation to us: much farther downstream and enterprise-operational; useful mainly as evidence that the market recognizes `Decision` as the primary value object.

## Market pattern

Adjacent products name one of three things:

1. **the value object** — Decisions / Decision Quality / Decision Intelligence / Decision Journal;
2. **the corrective stage/action** — Decision Framing / Frame Decisions;
3. **a memorable movement/transformation** — Frameback / Alter / Question Zero.

They generally do **not** expose an internal procedural label like `Decision Question Selection` as the product name.

Our strongest distinct wedge is narrower than generic `Decision Framing`:

> **select the question/uncertainty worth resolving now before deeper analysis or resource commitment.**

The capability can legitimately output `NO_REFRAME`; therefore names that imply always discovering a new question are structurally misleading.

## Naming implications

### `Decision Question Selection`
Keep as an internal capability label if useful. It is precise but sounds procedural/academic and unlike market-facing product names.

### `Question Discovery`
Weak as product name. Implies novelty/discovery and collides conceptually with Qzero's explicit question-first territory.

### `The Question Before the Question` / `Question Zero`
Do not pursue. Qzero already occupies this territory almost exactly.

### `Decision Framing`
Strong category language, but too broad as the product's differentiating name. DTrio and Frame Decisions already occupy this category directly.

### `Decision Focus`
Semantically attractive, but already an established GRC software company/product; avoid.

## Best current naming architecture

Do not force one string to carry brand, category, and capability.

Recommended separation:

- **Category:** `Decision Framing` or `Pre-Decision Decision Support`
- **Internal capability:** `Decision Question Selection`
- **Product brand:** should be short, memorable, positive, and encode either focus/leverage/order without claiming a provable global optimum.
- **Promise/tagline:** `Find the question worth resolving now.` / `Choose what to resolve before you spend to solve.`

## What remains unresolved

The market scan does not yet select the brand name. It narrows what the brand should NOT be:

- not a three-word procedural capability name;
- not another generic `Decision X` if it is indistinguishable from decision-intelligence products;
- not `Question Zero` / `question before the question` territory;
- not `Frameback`-like wording that risks looking derivative;
- not an `optimal/highest` claim unless prospective evidence can support it.

Next naming pass should generate short brand candidates from the unique movement:

```text
CURRENT QUESTION
→ SELECT CONTROLLING UNCERTAINTY
→ RESOLVE THAT FIRST
```

and then screen candidates for semantic fit, collision, pronunciation, memorability, and whether users infer the correct promise.
